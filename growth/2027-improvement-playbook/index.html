Start here

Build your annual plan

Use one setup session to make the first month actionable. Use January’s observations to finalize your targets.

1. Baseline · 20 min

List fixed commitments, near-term deadlines and constraints. Estimate usable hours from a representative week. Inventory existing obligations before adding a project.

2. Choose · 25 min

Set up to three year-end outcomes, with at most two projects active at once. Give every other module a maintenance floor or defer it explicitly.

3. Schedule · 15 min

Pick this month’s first deliverable, book the first work blocks and set a weekly review. Leave an open slot for spillover or recovery.

Example · Stabilize cash flow

Suppose essential spending including minimum payments averages $2,400 per month and the designated reserve holds $3,000. Runway is 1.25 months. A hypothetical two-month interim floor is $4,800, leaving a $1,800 gap. A verified surplus allocation of $300 per month would close that gap in six months, before interest or interruptions. Put known irregular bills in the forecast first. Check monthly statements to validate the surplus and revisit the allocation if the next 30 days fall below the cash floor.

Example · Build a skill with career evidence

Choose a common work task and a five-part rubric agreed with a reviewer. In week 1, spend one 50-minute block on a baseline attempt and rubric. In weeks 2 and 3, use each block for 10 minutes of recall, 30 minutes of independent work and 10 minutes of error review. In week 4, attempt a comparable new task before checking notes. A sample target is 4 of 5 criteria on a fresh case. Record the rubric, attempt, permitted artifact and reviewer feedback.

Make the trade-off explicit

Decision-making framework

Use the brief version for a reversible choice. Use the full version when money, commitments, health, employment, or other people’s data are materially affected.

  1. Frame the choice. Write what you must decide, by when, who owns it and what happens if you wait. Identify what the choice will displace.
  2. Apply hard limits. Set maximum time and cost, minimum safety and privacy requirements, approval needs and deadlines. Remove options that breach a hard limit. Mark any unverified limit as pending.
  3. Compare options. Include the current course and, when feasible, a small trial. Separate observed facts from assumptions. Weigh outcomes you value and record the most important unknown.
  4. Check robustness. Change the most disputed weight or score across a plausible range. If the preferred option changes, price the smallest test or piece of evidence that would resolve the difference. Recheck external rules with authoritative sources when relevant.
  5. Decide and revisit. Record the choice, rationale, downside cap, next action and review date. Set a trigger that would make you reopen the decision. If evidence arrives later, preserve the original log entry and append the update.
Time box. A five-minute decision needs a deadline, hard limit, small next action and review point. A consequential choice gets 30–60 minutes for the brief plus any necessary evidence-gathering. The options-and-values approach draws on a healthcare decision aid. Its use here is a general planning adaptation. [18]

How to decide whether it earns its keep

Estimate monthly net minutes as runs × (manual minutes − automated review minutes) − monthly upkeep. Divide one-time setup minutes by positive monthly net minutes for a rough break-even. For example, four runs saving 7 minutes each with 8 minutes of upkeep yield 20 minutes per month. A 40-minute setup breaks even in about two months. Replace the estimates with observed results after the pilot.

Write the trigger, owner, inputs, allowed outputs and stop switch. Test success, missing inputs, retries and duplicates with a sample. Where practical, run alongside the manual process for two cycles. Log run date, source ID, status and exceptions. If a serious failure occurs or manual review exceeds the time saved, pause and revert. Keep sending messages, deleting records, spending money, and sharing sensitive information behind explicit controls.

Research notes

Evidence and sources

The evidence supports the underlying practices to different degrees. The planning intervals, time budgets, metric thresholds, and automation pilots in this playbook are design choices to test in your circumstances.

PracticeWhat the evidence supportsHow far to apply it
Record progress and use if–then plansA meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies found that increasing progress monitoring improved attainment on average. A separate meta-analysis found benefits from pre-planning the response to a specific situation.Record evidence and plan for a likely obstacle. The findings span many goals and contexts, with no single review interval established here. [1] [2]
Plan time and protect a few work blocksA time-management meta-analysis reported moderate associations with job performance, academic achievement and well-being.The underlying evidence includes correlational studies. Judge the usefulness of protected blocks by completed outputs and schedule fit. [3]
Practice career skills and get feedbackJob-search intervention studies support combining search-skill building with motivation support. A separate mentoring review found generally small favourable associations across several outcomes.Apply job-search evidence when changing roles. For internal advancement, treat an agreed project and mentor feedback as experiments shaped by local expectations. [4] [5]
Recall, space and test learningReviews support practice testing and distributed practice for learning and retention. The spacing that works best varies with how long you need to retain the material.Use delayed recall and independently scored work. Test transfer separately on a new task. [7] [8] [19]
Build habits around a stable cueA small real-world study using self-reports found that repeating an action in a stable context can build automaticity over time, with substantial variation by person and behaviour.Keep the cue stable and observe the routine over time. Treat the two-week review as a fit check. Use population health guidance as context. [13] [14]
Budget, preserve liquidity and compare debt termsFCAC guidance supports tracking income and spending, building an accessible reserve, and evaluating debt repayment choices against real terms and affordability.Use the official guidance as a starting point. Verify current rules and individual terms before a 2027 tax, benefit, investment or debt decision. [9] [10] [11] [12]

Government cyber guidance supports account protection and backups.Pilot your structure with real lookup and restore tasks. The proposed folder and task conventions are operational choices.
