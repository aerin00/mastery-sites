Scope and standing conventions#

This runbook holds the practitioner's fixed standard: tropical zodiac, whole-sign houses, traditional (domicile) rulerships only, Hellenistic-primary with medieval/Persian material admitted only when explicitly labelled. Every technique is tagged by provenance. Where a stage in the original 12-step draft is a modern invention or lacks classical warrant, that is said plainly.

Two structural corrections drive the revised order. First, the ancient sources begin universal/whole-life analysis (length-of-life, eminence, temperament) BEFORE topical delineation; the draft inverts this. Second, predictive time-lords are meant to be layered in a HIERARCHY, not run as co-equal parallel tracks. The draft's stages 8 (progressions/directions) and 9 (returns) are also largely outside Hellenistic scope and are demoted and flagged below. The revised running order is at the end.

Sourcing honesty note: primary-source page/chapter references are given where obtainable. Many primary texts here are being read through modern translations and secondary scholarship (Brennan, George, Dykes, Schmidt, Riley, Robbins, Pingree); where I rely on a secondary source describing a primary one, I say so. Claims I could not attach to a citation are labelled Unverified.

Stage 1: Birth data and provenance#

Astrolog operation and evidence

Modern tool

Create one subject-specific .as input from the verified birth record, rather than reusing another person's default chart. Record local civil time, resolved UTC, explicit DST offset, longitude/latitude, calendar interpretation, source grade, and uncertainty separately. Astrolog's atlas and timezone tables are lookup aids, not documentary birth-time evidence. Prefer explicit DST input (-qb or -qc) to an inherited autodetect setting. Check the printed date, zone, and coordinates before reading positions.

For uncertain times, calculate at the earliest plausible time, central estimate, and latest plausible time. Track changes in Ascendant sign/degree, Sun altitude, Fortune/Spirit signs, and downstream time-lords. Never average conflicting sects into one unqualified reading. Preserve the three inputs and outputs.

Purpose. Establish the evidentiary grade of the birth time before any calculation, because the Ascendant degree, sect near the horizon, and every time-lord count depend on it.

Inputs. Claimed date, time, place; documentary source; jurisdiction and era.

Ordered operations.

  1. Record the source document type (birth certificate/hospital record, baby book, family memory, biography, rectified).
  2. Assign a Rodden Rating.
  3. Resolve time zone, LMT, DST/wartime offsets, and calendar system (Julian/Gregorian).
  4. Flag rounding bias.
  5. Set a confidence interval on the Ascendant.

Rodden Rating (as maintained on AstroDatabank). Modern

  • AA: from birth certificate, civil record, or family record recorded at birth. "The most accurate obtainable."
  • A: from the person, family member, or associate (from memory). "Usually accurate, but there are exceptions."
  • B: from a biography or autobiography.
  • C: "Caution, no source." Undocumented, ambiguous quote, or unverified reference. ALL rectified data are rated C.
  • DD: "Dirty data," conflicting/unreliable/dubious quotes.
  • X: date without a time.
  • XX: no reliable date.

AstroDatabank's own Help:RR guidance is explicit: AA, A, and B data are the only data that should be used in astrological studies. Data rated AA (from birth certificate or birth record) are the most accurate obtainable. Data rated A (from memory) are usually accurate, but there are exceptions. The AA grade was a later addition: per Wikipedia's Astrodatabank entry, originally the highest rating for accuracy was A, and the AA category was included at Marion March's suggestion to distinguish between data based on personal memory and that supported by a birth certificate.

Decision rules and thresholds.

  • If the rating is C/DD/X, do not make degree-of-Ascendant or sect-near-horizon claims without rectification and an explicit uncertainty note.
  • Rounding bias: recorded times cluster on :00, :15, :30. A time reading "born at 6:00" should be treated as an interval (roughly plus/minus 15 to 30 minutes), which at average rates moves the Ascendant by several degrees and can change the rising sign. Treat any round time as low-precision.
  • Timezone/DST traps: wartime clock changes, double summer time, and pre-standard-time LMT are the most common silent errors. The tz (Olson) database is reliable for the modern era but has known gaps for pre-1970 and pre-standardization local times.
  • Calendar: for births before the local Gregorian adoption, confirm Old Style vs New Style dating.

Failure modes and QC.

  • Accepting an atlas default timezone without checking historical DST.
  • Treating a rectified time as if it were AA.
  • Silently rounding a parent's approximate memory into a false-precision Ascendant.

Do the ancient sources address data quality? Partly. Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos III.2 (on the degree of the horoscopic point), explicitly raises that the exact moment of birth is hard to capture and discusses instruments (the astrolabe and water clock) and the adjustment via the moment of conception. Valens repeatedly collected and cross-checked charts against known life events (his Anthology is built from example nativities used this way). A data-quality consciousness is therefore genuinely ancient (Hellenistic), even though the Rodden apparatus is Modern.

Astrolog operation and evidence

Modern tool

  1. Load the validated birth input, then apply the explicit traditional settings. The order matters because .as files may contain settings as well as birth data.
  2. Export the seven planets' longitudes, latitudes, and absolute velocities, plus the actual Ascendant/MC and whole-sign cusps. In Whole Sign, the first cusp is the start of the rising sign, not the Ascendant degree; the tenth cusp is not necessarily the MC.
  3. Record tropical/geocentric/apparent settings, node choice, house system, ephemeris resources, engine version, exact command, and output timezone. Mean node (_Yn) is the reproducible example default here; a declared true-node study uses =Yn consistently.
  4. Independently re-enter birth data in the second calculator. Compare seven-planet longitudes, Ascendant/MC, and selected node using the same conventions. Log discrepancies and resolve them before interpretation. Arcminute agreement is an input/settings QC threshold, not proof of historical time accuracy.
  5. Recompute Lots and sign houses from the saved coordinates. Preserve the raw output alongside the comparison table.

Do not let a broad wheel or influence report silently introduce modern rulerships, minor aspects, alternative house systems, or numeric dignity scores. Display precision does not increase birth-time precision.

Purpose. Produce an accurate chart and independently verify it.

Inputs. Verified birth data from Stage 1; Astrolog as primary calculator; a separately configured second calculator, with backend independence recorded.

Ordered operations.

  1. Compute with Astrolog using the explicit traditional profile and ephemeris files; record backend and date coverage.
  2. Cross-check positions, Ascendant, and MC in a second calculator; disclose if both share Swiss Ephemeris.
  3. Set true equinox of date; geocentric; apparent positions.
  4. Decide true vs mean node.
  5. Compute derived points (Lots, bounds, decans, triplicities, phases, antiscia, fixed stars/parans).

Swiss Ephemeris. Modern The standard. Per Astrodienst's official documentation, the compressed version agrees with the JPL Ephemeris to 1 milli-arcsecond, and since the inherent uncertainty of the JPL ephemeris for most of its time range is a lot greater, the Swiss Ephemeris should be completely satisfying even for computations demanding very high accuracy. Since version 2.0 (2014) it is based on JPL DE431 (and DE441 for extended range); before 2014 it used DE406.

Two implications for practice: (a) JPL releases differ from each other, so sub-arcsecond precision is not objectively meaningful for most historical dates because the underlying ephemeris uncertainty is larger; (b) none of this matters for astrology, where interpretive orbs are degrees, not arcseconds. The verification value of a second engine is to catch timezone, house-system, and data-entry errors, not to improve astronomical precision.

Coordinate systems and what the ancients used.

  • Ancient astrologers used geocentric, apparent, ecliptic-longitude positions. This matches the standard astrological convention (true equinox of date).
  • True vs mean node: sources are not decisive; contemporary practice splits (Unverified which the ancients preferred). Whole-sign placement of the node rarely differs between true and mean, so at the sign level the choice seldom matters; at the degree level (aspects, bounds) it can.

House assignment. Whole-sign houses are the topical frame (Hellenistic). The quadrant systems (Porphyry earliest and simplest; Alcabitius, Regiomontanus, Placidus later) originated to measure planetary STRENGTH/activity ("dynamic division"), not to assign topics. Robert Schmidt's argument (Contemporary reconstruction) and Robert Hand's booklet hold that Valens used whole sign for topic and a quadrant division (probably Porphyry) for strength. The double-house reading (whole-sign for topic, quadrant for dynamic strength) is defensible and has partial precedent in Morinus (Renaissance; Astrologia Gallica Book 18, "On the Strengths of the Planets"). Brennan holds that whole-sign should remain the primary basis.

QC. In whole-sign the MC need not equal the 10th cusp; it can fall in the 9th to 11th whole-sign house and is read as an additional sensitive point.

Stage 3: Natal architecture#

Astrolog operation and evidence

Modern tool

Use the horizon listing (-Z) to inspect the Sun's altitude when checking day/night status; retain the horizon/refraction assumptions and run the birth-time interval near sunrise or sunset. Do not infer sect from the Sun's whole-sign house alone. Keep the actual angles alongside topical whole-sign houses; calculate a separate, labelled quadrant chart only if assessing dynamic strength.

Build sign-based configurations from the seven planets' signs first. Astrolog's degree-orb aspect lists (-a or -g) are a secondary check and can omit a valid whole-sign configuration. Determine aversion, superior position, joys, and sect membership explicitly in the worksheet. Do not read an influence ranking as a Hellenistic testimony ranking.

Sect, angularity, aspects and configuration.

Purpose. Build the structural skeleton before condition and synthesis.

Sect determination. Hellenistic Day chart if the Sun is above the horizon (whole-sign houses 7 to 12 broadly, or precisely above the Asc-Desc degree axis); night if below.

Boundary case: when the Sun is within a few degrees of the Ascendant or Descendant, sect is uncertain. Determination is by the degree of the Sun relative to the degree of the horizon, not by sign. Practitioners apply a 3 to 5 degree twilight tolerance and then judge by the whole chart. This is a genuine QC risk: a 15 to 30 minute time error near dawn or dusk can flip sect and invert the entire benefic/malefic-of-sect analysis.

Sect assignments. Hellenistic

  • Sect light: Sun by day, Moon by night.
  • Diurnal sect: Sun, Jupiter, Saturn. Nocturnal sect: Moon, Venus, Mars. Mercury by phase (oriental/morning = diurnal, occidental/evening = nocturnal).
  • Benefic of sect: Jupiter by day, Venus by night (the more helpful benefic). Malefic of sect: Saturn by day, Mars by night (the less harmful malefic). The out-of-sect malefic is the more dangerous one (Mars by day, Saturn by night).

Angularity and the ranking of places. Hellenistic

  • Pivots/angles (1, 10, 7, 4) strongest; succedents (11, 5, 2, 8) middling; cadents (3, 9, 12, 6) weakest for producing effects.
  • Valens' advantageous or busy places (chrematistikoi topoi): Anthology Book IV, Ch. 11 (Riley trans., p. 79) names the operative places. Valens' text: "The operative and effective signs are the Ascendant, MC, [the XI Place of the] Good Daimon, [the V Place of] Good Fortune, the Lot of Fortune, Daimon, Love, Necessity. Signs of moderate activity are [the IX Place of] the God, [the III Place of] the Goddess, and the other two angles. The rest of the signs are mediocre or bad."
  • The 6th, 8th, and 12th are the bad places; the 2nd is ambivalent.

Joys. Hellenistic Mercury joys in 1, Moon in 3, Venus in 5, Mars in 6, Sun in 9, Jupiter in 11, Saturn in 12. The joys underlie the naming of several places (Good Spirit = 11 = Jupiter's joy; Good Fortune = 5 = Venus's joy; God = 9 = Sun's joy; Goddess = 3 = Moon's joy; Bad Spirit = 12; Bad Fortune = 6).

Secondary reference frames. Use the Lot of Fortune and the Lot of Spirit as alternate ascendants and turn the chart from them (derived houses). Also turn the chart from any significator to read its topic (10th from the Lot of Fortune; houses from the 7th for the spouse). Hellenistic

Aspects and configuration. Hellenistic Aspects are conceived as SIGHT or scrutiny between whole signs, not as modern degree-orbs. Antiochus and Porphyry define the doctrine (Antiochus is largely preserved through Porphyry's Introduction and Rhetorius).

  • Configuration by sign: signs in sextile/square/trine/opposition see each other; signs in aversion (the 2nd, 6th, 8th, 12th from each other, 30 or 150 degrees) do not see each other.
  • Configuration by degree (with rays): a tighter, degree-based overlay used for bonification and maltreatment.
  • Bonification and maltreatment (Antiochus/Porphyry list, systematized by George in Ancient Astrology Vol. 1, Part Five, chs. 42 to 53): overcoming (a superior square/trine from the tenth-sign side, dominating from the right); hurling or striking with rays; spear-bearing (doryphory); adherence or connection (application within roughly 3 degrees); enclosure or besiegement (by body, or by ray, usually within about 7 degrees, with a third planet's intervening ray breaking it); and containment by sign. Benefics bonify; malefics maltreat, worse when out of sect.
  • Overcoming: the planet in the earlier sign (the superior/tenth-place position) has the upper hand.

Aversion and its mitigation. Hellenistic A planet in aversion to its own domicile, or to a significator, is disconnected. Mitigations include antiscia (a shadow connection), co-presence or bonds (same sign), and reception by rulership.

QC gate. Sect fixed and defensible; angular and advantageous places mapped; aspects-by-sign drawn before any degree-based refinement.

Stage 4: Planetary condition and rulership#

Astrolog operation and evidence

Modern tool

Populate a seven-planet worksheet from Astrolog: longitude/sign, topical house, velocity/direction, solar separation, and relevant configurations. Apply Egyptian bounds, Dorothean day/night/participating triplicity rulers, and the traditional domicile map from a named table. Verify exact table boundaries rather than copying unexplained dignity letters from the output.

Compute solar separation as the smaller angular distance; record the chosen beams/combustion/cazimi thresholds. A separation threshold is not an observed heliacal rising or setting. Visibility also depends on latitude, atmosphere, brightness, and horizon conditions. Label visibility inferred from elongation alone as a proxy.

Use event searches and nearby snapshots to establish stations and applications/separations. For ancient lunar void-of-course, search until the Moon has advanced the next 30 degrees and inspect exact major aspects to the other six classical planets, including across sign boundaries. A current aspect list or a next-sign-ingress cutoff cannot establish this condition. Derive waxing/waning and dispositor chains explicitly.

Purpose. Grade each planet's essential and accidental condition and establish rulership chains.

Essential dignities. Hellenistic core, later systematized.

  • Domicile (rulership), exaltation, triplicity, bound/term, decan/face. Domicile and exaltation are strongest.

Triplicity rulers, Dorothean scheme (Carmen Astrologicum Book I), three per triplicity (day / night / participating):

Triplicity Signs Day Night Participating
Fire Aries, Leo, Sagittarius Sun Jupiter Saturn
Earth Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn Venus Moon Mars
Air Gemini, Libra, Aquarius Saturn Mercury Jupiter
Water Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces Venus Mars Moon

Ptolemy (Tetrabiblos I.18, "Of the Triangles"; chapter numbering varies I.18 vs I.19 by edition) reduces this to two rulers per triplicity and, on the Water triplicity specifically, makes Mars ruler by both day and night with Venus (day) and Moon (night) as co-rulers on account of the sect and the femininity of the signs. Most Hellenistic practitioners (Brennan, George) use the Dorothean scheme; George (Ancient Astrology Vol. 1, ch. 15) emphasizes the first/sect lord for rejoicing. The triplicity lords of the sect light judge the fortune of successive periods of life; Dorotheus divides life into two parts (first lord, then second lord) with the third cooperating throughout. The clean "three thirds" division is a later Perso-Arabic refinement (via al-Andarzaghar and al-Khayyat). Medieval-Persian

Bounds/terms. Egyptian bounds are the older, dominant set (used by Dorotheus, Valens, Firmicus, Paulus, and nearly all surviving Greek horoscopes) and are preferred by Hellenistic practitioners. Brennan calls them by far the most widespread and popular set among the Greco-Roman astrologers; recent Jones/Steele scholarship traces the set to earlier Mesopotamian material. Ptolemy's own bounds (Tetrabiblos I.24, which he claimed to have found in an old manuscript) became popular only in the medieval-to-Lilly stream and never displaced the Egyptian set in Hellenistic practice.

Recommendation: use Egyptian bounds. Note Valens varies bound rulers by sect in some passages.

Almuten / dignity scoring. Numeric scoring of who has most dignity at a degree (al-Biruni, Ibn Ezra) is a Medieval-Persian formalization resting on the Hellenistic dignity categories.

Debility.

  • Fall (opposite of exaltation): genuinely Hellenistic. Greek hypsoma (exaltation) is paired with tapeinoma (fall/depression), located diametrically opposite; both derive from Babylonian antecedents (Ossendrijver, JHA 2025). Attested across Dorotheus, Ptolemy, Valens.
  • Detriment/exile (opposite of domicile): the PHENOMENON has Hellenistic roots (Anubio, paraphrasing Dorotheus, uses the participle diametron, "diametrical to his own domicile"; Rhetorius uses the Greek enantioma, "opposition/contrariety"), but it was NOT one of the routinely listed essential dignities in early Hellenistic practice. The dedicated term detriment (Latin detrimentum, which per Benjamin Dykes really means "to wear something out" and does not mean "the opposition of something") is a later Latin coinage; the established doctrine matured in the Medieval-Persian tradition (by Abu Ma'shar). This is a genuine live dispute. Label: detriment as a formal dignity = Medieval-Persian; the underlying observation = late Hellenistic (Rhetorius).

Solar phase conditions. Hellenistic with medieval refinement of thresholds.

  • Under the beams: within roughly 15 degrees of the Sun (some sources 17). Weakened, invisible.
  • Combustion: the tighter, more damaging band. Sources disagree: Hellenistic authors are cited near 8 to 9 degrees; George reports medieval authors using up to 12; Brennan states he uses 8 degrees in his own practice.
  • Cazimi (heart of the Sun): the tightest and FORTIFYING, not debilitating. Two definitions: per Kelly Surtees, the tightest definition is when a planet is within 17 minutes of the Sun and comes from the medieval astrologers; the second definition, from the Hellenistic period, defines a planet cazimi when it is up to one degree either side of the Sun.
  • Heliacal rising/setting and phasis (a planet's first or last visibility, and stationing near the Sun) carry special weight. Oriental (rising before the Sun, morning star) vs occidental (setting after the Sun, evening star) changes the expression and, for Mercury and Venus, governs the morning/evening star distinction and Mercury's sect.

Speed and direction. Hellenistic Stations and retrogradation. Retrograde planets read as functioning contrary to intention, hesitant, or turned inward; the two stations (first = turning retrograde, second = turning direct) are potent. A planet is strong when advancing and adding speed.

Lunar condition. Hellenistic Waxing (adding light) vs waning; applications and separations; collection and translation of light.

Void of course by the ANCIENT definition (kenodromia, "running in emptiness"): the Moon completes no exact Ptolemaic aspect within the next 30 degrees, regardless of sign boundaries (Antiochus, Dorotheus, Firmicus; George, Ancient Astrology Vol. 1, pp. 323 to 325). This is rare, a few times a year. The MODERN definition (no aspect before leaving the current sign, occurring every 2 to 3 days) is a Medieval change (Masha'allah onward) later codified by Lilly (Renaissance). Do not substitute the modern definition in Hellenistic work.

Rulership chains. Hellenistic

  • Domicile-lord dispositor chains: follow each planet to its domicile lord until you reach a planet in its own sign or a loop.
  • Key rulers: ruler of the Ascendant (the steersman of the nativity), ruler of the sect light, lord of the prenatal syzygy.
  • "Ruler of the whole chart" / almuten of the figure: a SINGLE overall chart ruler determined by scoring is a Medieval-Persian idea (almuten figuris). Hellenistic practice uses several distinct rulers for different purposes rather than one master ruler. Do not import a single chart ruler and call it Hellenistic.

QC gate. Every planet graded for dignity, solar phase, speed, and (Moon) phase and course; dispositor chains drawn; the correct bounds and triplicity tables used consistently.

Stage 5: Whole-chart synthesis (universal analysis)#

Astrolog operation and evidence

Modern tool

Assemble the whole-chart worksheet from the verified sect, actual angles, condition table, sect-light rulers, and checked Lots. Every statement about vitality, temperament, or eminence must point to those measured inputs and the selected interpretive method. Built-in interpretations (-I) and influence scores (-j) are not the synthesis authority. Astrolog's ability to calculate a point does not validate a lifespan estimate; retain the existing symbolic-vitality and client-care boundaries.

Purpose. Read the chart as a whole for life-quality, vitality, temperament, and eminence BEFORE topical detail. This is where the original draft order is wrong: the ancients front-load universal judgment.

Documented order of operations in the sources.

Ptolemy Tetrabiblos Book III proceeds in a deliberate topical sequence and puts LENGTH OF LIFE first among post-natal inquiries, stating (III.10, Robbins trans.): "The consideration of the length of life takes the leading place among inquiries about events following birth, for, as the ancient says, it is ridiculous to attach particular predictions to one who, by the constitution of the years of his life, will never attain at all to the time of the predicted events." He then treats bodily form and temperament, injuries and diseases, quality of soul (III.13 to III.14), then moves outward to fortune and dignity, and Book IV for material and relational topics.

Valens (Anthology Books II to IV) works through the places, the lords, the Lot of Fortune and its ruler, the master of the nativity, eminence, and then timing (profections, releasing).

Length-of-life doctrine (aphesis / hyleg-alcocoden). Hellenistic in origin (Ptolemy's apheta/releaser and anareta/destroyer; Dorotheus Carmen III; Valens Anthology III), later Persianized as hyleg/alcocoden.

  • Candidates for the releaser or hyleg (Ptolemy III.10): Sun, Moon, Ascendant, Lot of Fortune, prenatal syzygy, restricted to hylegiacal or aphetic places (Ptolemy accepts 1, 11, 10, 9, 7 of an idiosyncratic equal division; Dorotheus notably does not permit the 9th). The alcocoden (giver of years) is the planet with most dignity over the releaser's place, contributing its greater, mean, or minor years by angularity.
  • Note Ptolemy has only an apheta and anareta, with NO alcocoden or governor; the alcocoden year-allotment is elaborated in the Perso-Arabic tradition (Al-Biruni, Bonatti, Ibn Ezra). Medieval-Persian
  • This was historically the FIRST step. Modern ethics: most contemporary traditional practitioners do NOT predict death or lifespan; they read the hyleg as the chart's vital signature and skip the numeric year-allotment. Handle as symbolic, not as a clock.

Eminence and rank. Hellenistic Valens' criteria: Lot of Fortune and its lord angular and well-connected to the luminaries and benefics make for brilliant, fortunate, notable individuals, especially if angular and aspecting the Moon; the Lot in a cadent house produces an abandoned, unreared nature. The four principal lots (Fortune, Spirit, Basis, Exaltation) and their lords mark rank, especially the lord of one of these lots co-present with it. Ptolemy on the quality of the soul (III.13 to III.14) via Mercury and the Moon. Zodiacal Releasing from Spirit (Stage 7) is the main tool for the magnitude and timing of eminence.

Temperament. From the sect light, the Ascendant and its lord, the season and qualities of the signs involved, and the Moon's phase. The four-humor temperament grid as a scored worksheet is more developed in the Medieval and Renaissance stream (Lilly).

Adjudicating contradictory testimony. The sources give few explicit conflict-resolution rules. The working principle is to weight by (a) dignity, (b) place strength and advantageousness, and (c) sect, and to require multiple concurring testimonies before affirming an outcome. There is no ancient formula that resolves conflicts arithmetically; this is judgment.

QC gate. Length-of-life/vitality and eminence assessed at the whole-chart level before any single topic is delineated.

Stage 6: Topical natal promise#

Astrolog operation and evidence

Modern tool

Use the Parts listing (-P) as a candidate calculation list, then check each selected Lot's formula. -YP 0 reverses only Parts flagged for reversal at night; it does not certify that every built-in Part matches Paulus, Olympiodorus, or the chosen author. -YP 1 suppresses reversal; -YP -1 forces reversal even by day, so it is not an automatic night-mode setting.

Recompute with all longitudes expressed from 0 to 360 degrees, normalizing with ((x % 360) + 360) % 360:

  • Fortune: day = Asc + Moon - Sun; night = Asc + Sun - Moon.
  • Spirit: day = Asc + Sun - Moon; night = Asc + Moon - Sun.

For all other Lots, record author, exact formula, reversal rule, input degrees, result, whole-sign house, and traditional lord. Check same-named Parts for formula differences. Produce a topic matrix with place, lord/condition, Lot/lord, natural significator, and promise verdict. Derive turned houses by sign counting; retain the original natal chart as the base record.

Purpose. For each life topic, establish whether the thing is PROMISED (signified at all).

Per-topic method. Hellenistic For each topic examine the quartet: (a) the relevant PLACE (whole-sign house), (b) its LORD (domicile ruler) and that lord's condition and place, (c) the relevant LOT and its lord, (d) the natural significator. Cross-check by turning the chart from the significator.

Standard significators. Hellenistic unless noted.

The Lots. Hellenistic The seven Hermetic lots are Fortune, Spirit, Eros, Necessity, Courage, Victory, Nemesis. The day/night reversal dispute. Paulus Alexandrinus and Olympiodorus (and the bulk of Hellenistic sources) REVERSE the formulae by night. Annual profections. Hellenistic Near-universal in the sources: Valens, Ptolemy, Dorotheus, Firmicus, Paulus, Manilius.

  • Whole-sign, one sign per year from the Ascendant. Age 0 = 1st (Asc sign); active house = 1 + (completed age mod 12), counting forward from the Ascendant sign.
  • The domicile lord of the profected sign is the LORD OF THE YEAR. Examine its natal condition, its transits, and its position in the current solar return.
  • Monthly profections require a declared subdivision convention. The inherited roughly-28-day shorthand is incomplete: twelve such periods total 336 days. Verify period boundaries against the chosen method before use.
  • Co-ruler or participating ruler: a planet in, or aspecting, the profected sign participates. Valens sometimes gives lordship to a planet BODILY in the profected sign over the sign's ruler.
  • The profected Ascendant is overlaid on the solar return (Stage 9); the year-lord anchors the year.

Zodiacal Releasing (ZR) from Spirit and Fortune. Hellenistic Valens Anthology Book IV, the unique source.

  • Release from the Lot of Spirit for career, action, and eminence; from the Lot of Fortune for body, circumstance, and material. Some also release from Eros; experimental.
  • Period structure L1 (largest) through L4 (finest), nested. L1 periods run in zodiacal order from the lot's sign; each sign's period length equals the ruling planet's minor years. L2 subdivides L1 in the same proportions, and so on.
  • Planetary period lengths (lesser or minor years, Valens): Saturn 30, Jupiter 12, Mars 15, Sun 19, Venus 8, Mercury 20, Moon 25. A sign takes its ruler's years; the seven sum to 129, the basis of the decennials.
  • Loosing of the Bond: use a verified period table for the selected ZR method. The v1 opposite-sign shortcut is not valid executable logic; Astrolog’s documented macro describes a six-sign jump upon returning to the starting sign. Compatibility with the chosen timing method remains Unverified until checked.
  • Peak periods: periods falling in the angular signs from the releasing lot (the angular triads), especially the 10th sign from the lot, mark high points of activity and eminence.
  • The Fortune/Spirit distinction (Valens): Fortune is the more embodied and circumstantial track; Spirit is the volitional and career track.

Decennials. Hellenistic Valens. Each planet rules 10 years 9 months (129 months distributed); sub-periods by the lesser years counted as months (Saturn 30, Moon 25, Mercury 20, Sun 19, Mars 15, Jupiter 12, Venus 8 = 129). Distinct from ZR.

Circumambulation / primary directions through the bounds. Hellenistic Ptolemy IV, Valens. The releaser (Asc, Sun, Moon, Fortune, or MC) is directed through the bounds; the bound lord is the distributor (Persian jarbakhtar or divisor), and a planet whose degree the releaser reaches is the partner or participating ruler. Ascensional or rising times of signs convert arc to time. Ptolemy's method (1-degree key, equatorial-based) and Valens' method (oblique ascensions, rising times of signs) differ. See Stage 8.

Firdaria. Medieval-Persian Abu Ma'shar, related material in Paulus, later Al-Biruni, Bonatti.

  • Day order (75 years): Sun 10, Venus 8, Mercury 13, Moon 9, Saturn 11, Jupiter 12, Mars 7, then Mean Node 3 and South Node 2.
  • Night order (75 years): Moon 9, Saturn 11, Jupiter 12, Mars 7, Sun 10, Venus 8, Mercury 13, then the nodes.
  • Each major period divides into 7 sub-periods in Chaldean order starting from the period lord (nodes usually not subdivided).
  • Dispute: the placement of the node periods in the NOCTURNAL sequence differs between authors. Per Augurine's firdaria documentation, the Abu Ma'shar/Schoener tables place the nodes at the end of the nocturnal sequence (after Mercury), whereas the Bonatti/Dykes nocturnal variant places them after Mars, affecting night charts from roughly age 39 onward.

Firdaria is NOT Hellenistic; admit it only labelled.

Layering: the hierarchy question. The sources do NOT tell you to run all systems as co-equal parallels. Abu Ma'shar (Persian Nativities IV, Dykes trans.) gives an explicit HIERARCHY of annual techniques (firdaria ranks above the solar return and its lord). In Hellenistic practice, profections and ZR are the primary framing systems; the lord of the year and the current ZR ruler set the agenda, and finer systems (monthly profection, ZR L3/L4, directions) refine timing within them.

Adjudication when systems disagree: prioritize the system whose scope matches the question (ZR-Spirit for career magnitude; profection lord-of-year for the year's dominant topic), require agreement for a confident call, and treat lone testimonies as weak.

QC gate. Lord of the year identified with its condition and transits noted; current ZR L1 to L2 (from both Spirit and Fortune) identified; loosing-of-bond and peak status checked; any medieval systems (firdaria) explicitly labelled.

Stage 8: Progressions and directions (honest historical status)#

Purpose. Apply directional timing IF used, with explicit provenance.

Honest status.

  • Primary directions are the classical descendant of the ancient aphesis or circumambulation (Hellenistic origin, Ptolemy Tetrabiblos III to IV; Perso-Arabic at-tasyir or athazir; systematized in the Renaissance by Regiomontanus, Cardano, Naibod, Placidus, Morin). This is the ONE directional method with genuine ancient warrant. Martin Gansten is the standard modern scholarly reference.
  • Secondary progressions (a day for a year) are Modern or early-modern. Popularized after Placidus and bloomed in the 17th to 19th centuries. NO classical Hellenistic warrant.
  • Solar arc directions are Modern (19th to 20th century). No classical warrant.

If directions are used, specify.

  • Primary directions in mundo (actual positions, mundane frame, including latitude) vs in zodiaco (ecliptical, latitude zeroed). They coincide only where latitude is negligible.
  • Key or rate: Ptolemy 1 degree = 1 year; Naibod 0 deg 59' 08" (the Sun's mean daily motion); solar arc = the true solar arc. Mixing keys misdates events by months to a couple of years across a life.
  • Projection method: Placidus semi-arc is the common classical choice; Regiomontanus and Campanus give different arcs. Software that computes primary directions correctly includes Janus, Morinus (free), and Solar Fire.

Recommendation for this practitioner: use primary directions and bound-circumambulation (Stage 7) as the directional layer; treat secondary progressions and solar arc as OUTSIDE the traditional standard (Modern; admit only if labelled and clearly separated). Say plainly: secondary progressions and lunar returns have no Hellenistic warrant.

Solar revolutions. Medieval-Persian Masha'allah, Abu Ma'shar; later Bonatti, Morin.

  • Abu Ma'shar was probably the first to write on the solar return chart proper (the Sun returning to its exact natal longitude); Masha'allah earlier discussed annual revolutions.
  • Read against the natal: the profected Ascendant and the lord of the year (Persian salkhuday, still the ruler of the profected Ascendant) anchor the year; the return is not read in isolation. Abu Ma'shar explicitly criticized reading the return as a standalone snapshot.

Do Hellenistic sources use solar returns? Minimally. Valens has a distinct solar-return style method (per Riley; implemented in Delphic Oracle software), but the developed return chart is a Persian contribution. Treat the return-chart apparatus as Medieval-Persian.

Lunar returns. Source status is weak. The monthly profection is the Hellenistic monthly tool; the lunar return chart as read today is essentially Modern or medieval-adjacent with little classical warrant. Say so.

Precession-corrected vs tropical returns, and relocation.

  • Precession-corrected returns are a Modern debate; the traditional default is a tropical return to the natal Sun longitude.

Purpose. Use transits to TRIGGER what the natal chart promises and the time-lords have released. Transits are subordinate.

The hierarchy. Hellenistic and medieval. Valens and the Persian authors treat transits as the finest, most subordinate layer; they activate what is already promised (Stage 6) and already released or profected (Stage 7). A transit to a place or planet that is neither a current time-lord nor natally promised carries little weight.

Which transits are given weight.

  • Transits of and to the lord of the year and the current ZR or firdaria rulers.
  • Transits to the natal angles, the Lot of Fortune, the sect light, and the natal positions of the active time-lords.
  • Ingresses into the profected sign of the year.
  • Stations on sensitive natal points.

The outer-planet question. Saturn is the outermost classical body and the boundary of the traditional system. Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto fall outside the domicile-rulership scheme entirely; a strict traditional practitioner assigns them no rulerships and either ignores them or uses them only as mundane or transiting markers. Practitioner opinion ranges from total exclusion (purist) to using them as non-ruling transiting triggers (pragmatic). This practitioner's no-modern-rulers standard implies: no Uranus, Neptune, or Pluto rulerships; at most, note their transits as non-ruling triggers, clearly flagged as outside the tradition.

Eclipses. Hellenistic and mundane. Ptolemy Tetrabiblos Book II treats eclipses primarily in MUNDANE or general astrology (nations, weather, regions), with timing keyed to the eclipse's position and ascensions. A developed NATAL eclipse doctrine (eclipses on natal points as personal predictors) is largely Modern; the ancient emphasis is mundane. Flag natal eclipse claims as Modern.

Orbs and moieties. The traditional degree-based moiety system (half-orbs assigned per planet, combining for an aspect's orb) is chiefly a Medieval and Renaissance (Lilly) apparatus. The Hellenistic model is primarily configuration-by-sign, with a tighter degree-based "with rays" overlay (roughly 3 degrees for application or adherence). Use sign-based configuration first; use tight degree orbs for application and perfection.

Purpose. Identify windows where multiple layers agree, and state the prediction so it can be checked.

Honest status of convergence. Requiring agreement across profection + ZR + firdaria + return + transit is a Contemporary reconstruction, not a single documented ancient procedure. The ancients DID combine testimonies and required multiple concurring significators (Valens repeatedly; Abu Ma'shar's explicit hierarchy of techniques), so the PRINCIPLE of corroboration is ancient; the specific multi-system stack scoring is modern.

What the sources say about confidence. Affirm an outcome only when several significators concur; a lone testimony is weak; contradictory testimonies lower confidence. No ancient arithmetic converts testimonies into a probability.

Practical scoring. Contemporary reconstruction Contemporary traditional practitioners weight (1) whether the natal chart promises it, (2) whether the relevant time-lord (lord of year, ZR ruler) is activated, (3) whether a directional or return testimony agrees, (4) whether a transit triggers it in a specific window. More independent layers agreeing means higher confidence and a tighter window.

Falsifiability. State the prediction as a testable claim: the topic, the direction (benefic or malefic), the window (dates), and the confidence. Log it before the window. This is the discipline that lets you check base rates and calibrate. Vague, non-dated, non-directional statements cannot be scored and should be avoided.

Note also the ascensional-time subtlety Robert Schmidt flags: a completion at the nth year corresponds to age n-1, so off-by-one errors in period arithmetic are a common QC failure.

Fate and agency: the philosophical frame.

  • Valens is broadly fatalistic (Stoic-inflected): the chart shows what fate decrees; astrology's value is equanimity in the face of it.
  • Ptolemy is a soft-determinist (Tetrabiblos I.3, the chapter arguing astrology is a possible and useful science): celestial causes are natural and conditional, modifiable by other causes; foreknowledge allows preparation and self-improvement, not moral passivity. This is the Aristotelian and naturalist wing versus Valens' Stoic wing.

Genuine scholarly disputes#

  1. Bounds table. Egyptian (older, dominant in Hellenistic horoscopes; preferred by Brennan and George) vs Ptolemaic (Tetrabiblos I.24, found in an old manuscript; popular in the Lilly and Bonatti stream) vs Chaldean (symmetric, sect-dependent, historically marginal). Recommendation: Egyptian for Hellenistic work.
  2. Lot of Fortune reversal by night. REVERSE (Paulus, Olympiodorus, Valens in practice, most Hellenistic sources; Brennan) vs DO NOT REVERSE (Ptolemy; Lilly; recently Wade Caves, Anthony Louis). Schmidt questioned reversal of the non-Fortune/Spirit Hermetic lots specifically. Recommendation: reverse for Hellenistic-primary practice.
  3. Quadrant vs whole-sign houses. Whole-sign for topics is the Hellenistic default (Brennan, George, Hand); quadrant divisions originated for STRENGTH or dynamic measurement (Schmidt's dynamic division); the double-house synthesis uses both (Morin precedent). Dispute over whether quadrant houses ever carried topics in antiquity (Brennan: no; Deborah Houlding contests aspects of the whole-sign-primacy narrative).
  4. Sect boundary near the horizon. Sign-based vs degree-based determination; how wide a twilight tolerance (0, 3, or 5 degrees) before calling a chart mixed. No settled ancient rule; degree-based is more precise but hostage to birth-time accuracy.
  5. Combustion thresholds. Under-the-beams roughly 15 (or 17); combustion roughly 8 (Brennan) to 9 (some Hellenistic) to 12 (medieval, per George); cazimi 17 arcminutes (medieval strict) vs about 1 degree (Hellenistic). Genuine source disagreement.
  6. Detriment's antiquity. Brennan (with Levente Laszlo) argues the concept originates in the earlier Hellenistic tradition (Anubio; Rhetorius' enantioma). Seven Stars Astrology argues it is essentially Medieval (Rhetorius formalizes, al-Andarzaghar develops, Abu Ma'shar establishes) and criticizes the coined term exile. Agreed by both: the Latin term detrimentum and the settled doctrine are later.
  7. Triplicity rulers. Dorothean three-ruler sect scheme (standard in Hellenistic practice) vs Ptolemaic two-ruler scheme (differs on Water: Mars day and night, with Venus and Moon as co-rulers). The downstream Lilly Mars-only Water table is a further Renaissance simplification and, per Anthony Louis, a misreading of Ptolemy.
  8. Nocturnal firdaria order. Abu Ma'shar, Al-Biruni, and Schoener sequence (nodes after Mercury) vs Bonatti's differing nocturnal sequence (nodes after Mars), affecting night charts from roughly age 39.
  9. Nodes. True vs mean; and whether to subdivide node firdaria periods.
  10. Returns. Precession-corrected vs tropical; birthplace vs relocation.

Bottom line up front#

  • Three categories, never blurred. Only three geocosmic-adjacent phenomena have real peer-reviewed support (calendar seasonality, lunar phase, and geomagnetic storms), and even those are contested or decaying. The entire body of classical financial astrology (Bradley siderograph, McWhirter's 18.6-year nodal method, Merriman's critical reversal dates, Gann, planet-to-sector significations) is documented and asserted but has no independent controlled testing and should be labelled Unverified.
  • The math is the real constraint, not the astrology. Multi-decade cycles that dominate financial astrology (Jupiter-Saturn 19.86y, Saturn-Neptune about 36y, Kondratieff 45 to 60y) afford only 2 to 9 independent observations in the entire reliable market record, so they are statistically unreachable regardless of how convincing the chart looks. With roughly 45 planetary pairs times 5 to 8 aspects times multiple orbs times assets times windows, the implicit hypothesis count runs into the thousands, demanding a t-statistic above 3.0 (Harvey, Liu and Zhu 2016), multiplicity correction, and cost-and-overfitting survival before anything graduates.
  • Actionable posture. Build the claims.yaml engine to reject any claim lacking a stated null and base rate, test only short-period (lunar, calendar, geomagnetic) claims for actual significance, assign near-zero position weight to unvalidated timing signals, and stay on the generic-advice side of the BCSC line if publishing from Vancouver.

Key findings#

1. Lunar phase. Empirically tested: mixed Dichev and Janes (2003) reported that returns in the 15 days around new moon dates are about double the returns in the 15 days around full moon dates, and that the pattern is pervasive across all major US stock indexes over the last 100 years and nearly all major indexes of 24 other countries over the last 30 years. Yuan, Zheng and Zhu (Journal of Empirical Finance 13(1), 2006) found a global equal-weighted new-minus-full-moon difference of 40.26 bps per 15-day window and 27.48 bps per 7-day window, that is 4.8% and 3.3% annualized, both significant at the 5% level and robust to volume, macro announcements, and other calendar anomalies. Later international work (2011) and out-of-sample checks find the effect weak in recent data and unlikely to survive transaction costs.

2. Geomagnetic storms. Empirically tested: supported with caveats. Krivelyova and Robotti (Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta Working Paper 2003-5b, October 2003) found that for most countries in their sample, the previous week's unusually high levels of geomagnetic activity have a negative and statistically and economically significant impact on stock returns. The proposed channel is mood-mediated (geomagnetic storms increase depression, which changes risk-taking); the mechanism and robustness remain debated.

3. Calendar seasonality (Sell in May). Empirically tested: supported Bouman and Jacobsen (American Economic Review 92(5), 2002, 1618 to 1635; sample January 1970 to August 1998) found the Sell in May effect present in 36 of the 37 countries in their sample. It survived out-of-sample: Jacobsen and Zhang found the Halloween effect present in all 37 countries for the out-of-sample period September 1998 to April 2011, and Andrade, Chhaochharia and Fuerst (2013) found stock returns about 10 percentage points higher in November-April half-year periods than in May-October half-year periods. Challenged by Maberly and Pierce (2004) as driven by the October 1987 crash and the August 1998 LTCM collapse.

4. Post-publication decay is a first-class hazard. McLean and Pontiff (Journal of Finance 71(1), 2016) found portfolio returns 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication. Any anomaly, geocosmic or not, must be assumed to weaken once known.

5. The classical financial-astrology canon is untested. Bradley's own text is the honest baseline: at no time must the reader gain the impression that a siderograph is a prediction of what the stock market will actually do, and a limitation of the siderograph is that it cannot be construed as a forecast of secular trends. Merriman reports his own hit rates; no independent peer-reviewed verification exists.

With S swings over T trading days, expected coverage of a plus-or-minus k window is approximately:

coverage ≈ S × (2k + 1) / T

For a market averaging a swing roughly every 20 trading days with a plus-or-minus 3 window (7 days), naive chance coverage is on the order of 30 to 35%. A claimed 60% hit rate is therefore far less impressive than it sounds.

The core stage. Economic / statistical cycle research

Multiple comparisons. With about 45 pairs times 5 to 8 aspects times several orbs times several assets times several windows, the implicit hypothesis count is in the thousands. Apply Bonferroni or Holm for familywise control and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR for discovery. For strategy selection use:

  • White's Reality Check. Halbert White, "A Reality Check for Data Snooping," Econometrica 68(5), 2000, 1097 to 1126.
  • Hansen's Superior Predictive Ability test. Peter R. Hansen, "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability," Journal of Business and Economic Statistics 23(4), 2005, 365 to 380.
  • Romano-Wolf stepdown. Romano and Wolf, "Stepwise Multiple Testing as Formalized Data Snooping," Econometrica 73(4), 2005, 1237 to 1282.

Raise the bar. Harvey, Liu and Zhu, "...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns," Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 2016, 5 to 68. A newly claimed factor needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a t-statistic greater than 3.0, not 2.0, given the factor zoo; they argue most claimed findings in financial economics are likely false.

Overfitting. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey and Lopez de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio," Journal of Portfolio Management 40(5), 2014, 94 to 107) corrects an observed Sharpe for multiple-testing selection bias, short samples, and non-normality. The Probability of Backtest Overfitting via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting," Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 2017, 39 to 69).

Event studies. Estimation window, event window, abnormal and cumulative abnormal returns, and the Boehmer-Musumeci-Poulsen standardized cross-sectional test for event-induced variance (Journal of Financial Economics 30(2), 1991, 253 to 272).

Astronomical events are cyclical and therefore clustered and overlapping; their windows are non-independent, violating the independence assumption of standard event-study t-tests. This is a specific and serious problem for geocosmic backtests.

Cross-validation. Purged and embargoed cross-validation and combinatorial purged CV (Marcos Lopez de Prado, Advances in Financial Machine Learning, Wiley 2018, ch. 7 and ch. 12). Purging removes training observations whose labels overlap in time with the test set; embargo drops training observations immediately after the test set.

Resampling. Circular block bootstrap for autocorrelated series; Monte Carlo nulls.

Costs. Any claimed edge must survive realistic transaction costs and slippage.

Gate. No claim graduates without multiplicity correction, t above 3, a cost model, and purged-CV survival.

Genuine disputes#

Lunar effect persistence and tradeability. For: Dichev and Janes (2003), Yuan, Zheng and Zhu (2006), significant, robust, 3 to 5% annualized. Against: 2011 international follow-ups and out-of-sample checks find it weak in recent data and unlikely to survive costs.

Geomagnetic effect. For: Krivelyova and Robotti (Atlanta Fed 2003), significant negative predictive impact. Against: mood-mediation mechanism and robustness across specifications are debated.

Solar cycle causation. For: Scafetta (2012; Scafetta and Bianchini 2022) and Stefani et al. (Solar Physics 2016 to 2021) argue Jupiter-Saturn tidal forcing synchronizes the 11-year Schwabe cycle (spring-tidal 9.93y and Jupiter 11.86y bounding an 11.07y resonance). Against: Nataf (2022), Cionco, and Okal and Anderson (1975) reject it. Tidal forces are far too small and no mechanism survives classical physics.

Kondratieff periodicity. For: long-wave proponents and Schumpeter's promotion. Against: Solomou, Maddison, Rostow. Small sample (4 to 5 cycles), data-mining and deterministic-detrending objections.

What the evidence actually supports#

Peer-reviewed support that broadly survives scrutiny.

  • Halloween or Sell in May seasonal effect (Bouman and Jacobsen 2002; out-of-sample confirmations by Jacobsen and Zhang and by Andrade, Chhaochharia and Fuerst 2013), though challenged on outlier sensitivity (Maberly and Pierce 2004).
  • Geomagnetic-storm effect on returns (Krivelyova and Robotti, Atlanta Fed 2003), with a plausible mood mechanism.

Peer-reviewed support that is mixed or has decayed.

  • Lunar new-moon and full-moon return difference: strong in original 100-year and 48-country samples (3.3 to 4.8% annualized), weaker and likely not cost-surviving out-of-sample.
  • Most calendar anomalies (January, day-of-week): decayed post-publication (McLean and Pontiff 2016).
  • Kondratieff long waves: contested, likely overstated.

Claims with NO controlled evidence.

Bradley siderograph, McWhirter nodal method, Merriman CRDs (self-reported only), Gann astrological methods, all planet-to-sector significations, the Benner cycle, and crypto genesis charts. These are documented and asserted, not tested.

Prior plausibility for astrological causation is low: Shawn Carlson's double-blind test (Carlson, "A double-blind test of astrology," Nature 318, 1985, 419 to 425) found astrologers performed no better than chance and concluded the experiment refuted the astrological hypothesis. Later re-analyses by astrology-affiliated authors dispute the statistics; these are advocacy re-analyses in astrology outlets, not independent confirmation.

Recommendations#

  1. Build the claims.yaml engine to reject any claim lacking a stated null and base rate (Stage 1 gate). This alone eliminates the majority of practitioner assertions from testing.
  2. Test only Tier A short-period claims for actual significance (lunar, calendar, geomagnetic, and any aspect cycle with a period under about 3 years). Treat all long-cycle geocosmic claims (Jupiter-Saturn and slower) as descriptive narrative only, never as validated signals. The power table forbids conclusions.
  3. Apply the full statistical discipline to everything that is testable. A t above 3 hurdle (Harvey, Liu, Zhu), Benjamini-Hochberg or Romano-Wolf multiplicity correction, Boehmer-Musumeci-Poulsen variance-robust event tests, purged and embargoed CV, circular block bootstrap, deflated Sharpe, and a realistic cost model. Log all forward forecasts and score them with Brier scores.
  4. Assign near-zero position weight to any signal that has not cleared Stage 10; use fractional (not full) Kelly and hard drawdown limits.
  5. If publishing from Vancouver, stay on the generic-advice side of the BCSC line (impersonal market commentary, no security-specific buy or sell recommendations tailored to individuals) and confirm current NI 31-103 status with counsel.

Benchmarks that would change these recommendations. A Tier A geocosmic claim that clears t above 3 after multiplicity correction, survives purged CV out-of-sample, and beats its base rate net of costs would justify a small non-zero position weight. Independent, pre-registered replication of any Merriman CRD or Bradley turning-point hit rate against a fixed swing-point definition would move those techniques from Unverified to a tested tier. Absent those, the posture stands.
