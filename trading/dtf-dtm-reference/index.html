DTF and DTM Reference

Field guide to Dynamic_Momentum_DTF.pine and Dynamic_Time_Map.pine: what every mark means, how to read the dashboards, and how the two fit together. Both scripts are ticker-agnostic; the BTC- originals are kept unchanged beside them.

Overview

DTF: Dual Time Frame Momentum

Answers which direction and when a pullback has ended. A higher-timeframe stochastic grants permission (long or short); a chart-timeframe stochastic crossover after a qualifying pullback arms a setup; a later close beyond the prior close is the price trigger. Based on Robert Miner's DTF rules (see claim MINER-HPT-001).

Pane: separate oscillator pane, with price markers forced onto the main chart.

DTM: Dynamic Time Map

Answers when the next turn is probable. It builds a confirmed pivot path, measures the calendar-day durations between pivots, and projects several historical duration models forward from the last confirmed pivot. Where the models overlap it issues ranked windows. Based on Miner's Time Rhythm Zones and Time Bands (see claim MINER-DT-030).

Pane: overlay on price. Intended for daily charts.

Division of labour. DTM never says which way price goes; DTF never says when a turn is due. A DTF setup that lands inside a DTM ranked window is the highest-quality condition either script can describe on its own.

Setup lifecycle

Cancellation order matters. On each bar the script checks, in order: data unavailable, invalidation breached, permission lost, chart-TF extreme reached, expiry, then follow-through. A bar that both breaches the invalidation and closes higher is a cancellation, not a trigger.

DTM: Dynamic Time Map

How a forecast is built

Scores are evidence counts, not probabilities. A window scored 4.97 had roughly five independent historical intervals pointing at it plus bonuses. It says nothing about how far price will move.

DTM visuals

Green / orange zigzag. The confirmed pivot path. Green legs advance, orange legs decline. Every projection is measured from these pivots, so if the path looks wrong the forecasts are wrong.
▣
"Confirmed L" / "Confirmed H" label. The most recent confirmed pivot and the anchor for the live forecast. Placed at the pivot's date, not the bar where it was confirmed. Optional "Show provisional leg" draws the unconfirmed leg from it.
Orange vertical bands. The ranked forecast windows for the next opposite pivot (up to 3, each ≤ 5 days). Darker means higher score. The label on each shows dates, width and score.
Orange dashed vertical, Maximum boundary. Latest date the historical maximum-duration model expects the turn by. Conditional on no other pivot confirming first, hence the asterisk in the table.
TRZ cycle (teal, top). Trimmed same-type duration range (high→high for a HIGH forecast) projected from the last same-type pivot. Miner's Time Rhythm Zone, broad on its own.
TRZ overlap (teal, narrower). Intersection of the same-type range with the directional range (low→high) projected from the anchor. The classic TRZ zone.
Time Band directional (magenta, wide). Range of the most recent directional durations (default last 10) projected from the anchor. Adapts faster than TRZ because it ignores older history.
Time Band cycle (magenta). Recent same-type durations projected from the last same-type pivot.
Time Band overlap (magenta, narrowest). Intersection of the two Time Band ranges. Ranked windows inside both this and the TRZ overlap earn the range bonus.
Grey bands (optional). "Earliest-window baseline": the same number of covered days placed as early as possible. Display-only comparison so you can see whether the model's windows differ from a naive placement.
Faded historical bands (optional). "Previous issued forecasts to display" shows earlier versions with their outcome text so you can eyeball hits and misses.

Detailed

The Compact rows plus the ingredients: detector in use; pivot date versus the date it became known and the lag in days; observation counts for the directional and cycle families against the minimum; how many each TRZ trim retained; the TRZ, Time Band, and shared overlap ranges as dates; which alternative anchors are on; the theoretical window; history counts; data gaps; lead and elapsed days; preset and adjacency mode; and the baseline text. Use it when a window looks odd and you want to see which family produced it.

Window evidence

One window dissected. Pick Strongest, Nearest remaining, or Window 1 to 3 under "Window to inspect".

  • Score components at peak: points + range bonus + maximum bonus + theory bonus, summing to the displayed score.
  • Directional unique / cycle unique: how many independent historical intervals voted. Five uniques from five different swings is stronger than five votes from anchor variants of one swing.
  • Primary / close / secondary: which anchor variant produced each vote, so you can see whether closing or secondary anchors are carrying the score.
  • Supporting sample IDs and anchor dates: the actual historical swings behind the window. Cross-check them on the chart.
  • Range support at peak: whether the peak day sits inside TRZ and Time Band, or is a "broad empirical overlap" with no point-supported peak.

Using DTF and DTM together

  1. Start with DTM. Note the next expected pivot type (HIGH or LOW), the nearest window, and whether it sits inside both overlaps. Set the Countdown as your attention timer.
  2. Check the DTF ribbon. If DTM expects a HIGH and the HTF ribbon is orange (Transition or Upper extreme), the two agree that a top is plausible. Teal with a HIGH forecast means the timing model is calling a pause inside a trend; treat it as an exit or tightening cue rather than a reversal.
  3. Wait for the DTF setup. An S inside a DTM window, in the direction of the expected turn, is the condition you are looking for. Note the orange invalidation line.
  4. Act on the trigger, not the setup. The T / DTF marker is the entry event. If no trigger comes within three bars the setup retires with a grey X and you wait for the next one.
  5. Manage with U1 / U2 or your own rules. The purple U2 line is the cycle stop; the aqua U1 line shows how a tight trail would have behaved. Neither places orders.
  6. Score the window afterwards. When the pivot confirms, DTM resolves the forecast. Over time the Scorecard's paired difference tells you whether the timing filter is earning its keep on this symbol.
What agreement looks like: DTM State "Active", DTF ribbon orange or freshly flipped, a DTF S then T in the forecast direction, and the setup's invalidation just beyond the recent extreme. What disagreement looks like: a DTF trigger with no DTM window for weeks, or a DTM window that passes with the DTF stochastic still mid-range and no cross.

Worked examples

Dates and prices are illustrative. Each example names the exact visual cue at every step so you can match it on screen.

Example 1: Long, trend continuation

  1. DTM. Panel reads Next LOW? | #361 · State: Upcoming · Nearest remaining 09-24 to 09-28 (4.1). Label on chart says Confirmed H. The map wants a bottom; the countdown is your alarm.
  2. DTF ribbon. Teal, table says Bullish · Middle. Longs permitted. A low inside the window would be a Table 2.2 row-1 continuation entry.
  3. Pullback. Aqua and orange fall below the dotted 50 while price drifts toward the window. Midpoint requirement met. No action.
  4. Setup. On 09-25, inside the orange DTM band and the teal TRZ overlap, aqua crosses above orange. A teal S prints at the bottom of the pane and the orange invalidation line appears on price one tick under the leg low. Stop level is now known.
  5. Trigger. Next bar closes above the prior close. Lime ▲ DTF under the bar, T in the pane. Enter long at that close.
  6. Manage. Yellow 2U; aqua U1 and purple U2 both at the invalidation. Scale half on U1 ✕ after the trail tightens; let U2 ride the cycle stop until the ribbon turns orange and a bearish daily cross arms it.
  7. Resolve. When the low confirms, DTM scores #361 as a hit.

Example 2: Short, top inside a window

  1. DTM. Next HIGH? | #360 · State: Active · Nearest remaining 09-11 to 09-15 (4.1), plus Maximum boundary 09-21 | conditional. Label says Confirmed L. Price is already inside the orange band.
  2. DTF ribbon. Orange, table says Bullish · Transition. Weekly is still bullish but leaving overbought, so with the default setting shorts are not permitted yet. Watch, do not act.
  3. Context flips. Two days later the confirmed weekly bar prints fast below slow. Ribbon turns red, table says Bearish · Transition, and an alert fires Confirmed context reversal. Shorts now permitted.
  4. Setup. Still inside the DTM window and before the maximum boundary, the daily aqua line, having been above 50, crosses below orange with fast still under 80. Red S at the top of the pane; orange invalidation line one tick above the leg high.
  5. Trigger. Within three bars a close prints below the prior close. Fuchsia ▼ DTF above the bar, T in the pane. Enter short.
  6. Manage. 2U marks entry with both stops at the invalidation. U1 trails one bar above price after two bullish daily crosses; U2 arms once the weekly reaches oversold.
  7. Resolve. The high confirms inside the window: DTM hit, and the next forecast (Next LOW?) is issued from it.

The short version: DTM says when, the ribbon says which way, S says ready, T says go, the orange line says out. Any step missing means no trade.

Caveats and known gaps

  • Both scripts were generalised from BTC versions on 2026-09-10. DTF dropped a base-currency gate; DTM's gate was already disabled. Neither has been compiled in TradingView since the change beyond your own paste.
  • Weekend gaps. DTM measures and projects in calendar days, as Miner does. On five-day markets a window can start or end on a weekend with no bar to score against. Faithful to the method, but it will depress hit rates slightly compared with 24/7 crypto.
  • Confirmation lag. A DTM pivot confirms only after depth bars plus the ATR deviation. The "Confirmed L" label can appear a week after the low. Forecasts issued from a late-confirmed pivot may already have windows in the past ("Issued after end").
  • HTF context is one bar behind by design. DTF reads the last closed HTF bar so it never repaints, which means the first bars of a new HTF trend are systematically missed.
  • Not the DT Oscillator. Miner's proprietary DTosc is unpublished. DTF uses a two-line stochastic at his illustrated 8/3/3 settings. Results describe that substitute.
  • Scores and hit rates are in-sample. Both dashboards recompute from loaded history with current inputs. Change an input and the Scorecard changes with it.
  • Illustrative management only. The 2U / U1 / U2 machinery models equal halves, one campaign at a time, with no position sizing, costs, or orders.
