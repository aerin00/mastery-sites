02Your testing routine

Repeat for each newly issued forecast.

STEP 1 · CAPTURE

Save the original forecast

At pivot confirmation, screenshot the chart. Record issuance date, forecast ID, target direction and window dates.

STEP 2 · WATCH

Observe the window

Watch for the expected turn as the window arrives. Apply your separate entry and confirmation rules.

STEP 3 · WAIT

Let the pivot confirm

A window expiring does not establish a miss. Wait for the next opposite pivot to be confirmed.

STEP 4 · COMPARE

Check the actual turn date

Compare the pivot’s occurrence date with the original windows, not the later confirmation date.

03Review without fooling yourself

Inputs → 7. Dashboard and review → Scorecard

Read the scorecard

  • Hit: the next confirmed opposite pivot occurred inside an originally issued window, including its boundaries.
  • Baseline: simple earliest windows with matching coverage.
  • Paired difference: model hit rate minus baseline hit rate on the same eligible forecasts.
  • Check the denominator: inspect pending, excluded and no-window cases alongside hits and misses.

What do excluded and no-window cases mean?

V1.2 separates data gaps, pending confirmation, turns occurring on or before issuance, and resolved cases without a window. No-window cases are abstentions. Its chart scorecard is descriptive and uses different inclusion rules from the separate frozen research comparison.

When should I use Window evidence?

Switch to Window evidence when you want to inspect what supports a window. Select Strongest, Nearest remaining or a numbered window in Inputs. Supporting counts describe the peak and may share underlying swings. The evidence score is not a probability.

Keep the original evidence.
Changing settings, reloading or changing available history reconstructs the scorecard. It retains at most 100 forecast versions.
