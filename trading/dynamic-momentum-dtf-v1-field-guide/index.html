Why can an overbought context still allow a long?

By default, a bullish context can support continuation while overbought. Long permission requires context fast above slow and both above the lower threshold. Short permission requires fast below slow and both below the upper threshold. The optional conservative switch blocks longs when both context lines are at the upper extreme, and shorts when both are at the lower extreme.

TLater higher close triggers

Within the next 3 bars, a close above the immediately previous bar’s close qualifies, provided all checks still pass.

Cancellation wins over follow-through. The script checks unavailable data, invalidation, lost momentum permission and the chart fast line reaching its directional extreme before allowing a trigger. No same-bar S → T. Bar 3 can still trigger; otherwise the setup expires.

This panel explains the default rules, not a live signal. “Higher close” does not mean a break above the setup bar’s high. The short case mirrors the comparison.

Pullback modes and the invalidation reference

Midpoint pullback: corrective-leg fast must go strictly below 50 for long, or above 50 for short. Any corrective crossover: removes that depth requirement. Extreme pullback: requires both chart lines to have reached the relevant extreme together during the leg.

The default reference is the corrective leg’s lowest wick minus one tick for long, or highest wick plus one tick for short. The leg includes the crossover bar. A wick touching or crossing that padded level cancels the pending setup. “Closing price” instead uses the leg’s lowest/highest close and requires a close strictly beyond it. A tick is the symbol’s minimum price increment, not a percentage.

04Your TradingView testing routine

Keep the first pass focused on setups and triggers.

1. Check the table first

Verify the feed, timeframe pair, engine, confirmed context and HTF close time. “Armed long/short” means a setup is pending. “Last trigger / invalidation” is historical, not necessarily active protection.

2. Capture S and its reference

Screenshot the setup candle, context state and invalidation level. Note when the next three chart bars will finish.

3. Record T, × or expiry

Advance one closed bar at a time. Record what actually happened, including cancelled and expired setups. The status table’s Last event helps explain the transition.

4. Evaluate separately

A T confirms this script’s condition, not a profitable trade. Record subsequent price behavior using one consistent evaluation rule. This indicator has no performance scorecard or Strategy Tester results.

Before counting a test

Checklist state resets on reload. This page does not save a trading journal.

No signals? Check these first.

Only standard BTC/XBT charts at 1H, 4H or Daily are supported. Warm-up, missing data, a flat stochastic range, blocked context, an incomplete momentum leg or an unqualified pullback can prevent setups. A crossing alone does not guarantee S, and S does not guarantee T.

Other engines and custom settings

Smoothed RSI, Stochastic RSI and Directional Efficiency are experimental adaptations. They are not the proprietary DT Oscillator. Defaults use 8/3/3 smoothing; Smoothed RSI and Directional Efficiency use 30/70 extremes, while Stochastic and Stochastic RSI use 20/80. RSI range length is used only by Stochastic RSI. Compare one change at a time.

05Optional tools, after the first pass

Two-unit management

Enable “Show two-unit management” to inspect illustrative equal halves. It tracks one campaign at a time; not every T starts a new campaign.

Read 2U, U1 and U2 correctly

2U: a tracked campaign begins when idle and a price trigger occurs. Both halves begin from the setup’s invalidation reference.

U1, aqua: uses cycle protection, then tightens after two opposing chart-momentum reversals. U2, purple: becomes armed after the confirmed context reaches the trade-direction extreme; a later opposing reversal tightens it. A same-bar newly reached extreme does not count as that later reversal.

Crossovers tighten protection; price breaches create exit markers. Existing protection is checked before close-decided updates, which apply to the next bar. The plotted lines show active protection; the table shows next protection. Once tight, protection is wick-based even if the initial basis was Closing price. These are illustrative references, not executable stops or fill prices.

Alerts and Time Map

Which alert events should I follow?

Use “Long setup” / “Short setup” for early notice, and “Long price trigger” / “Short price trigger” for follow-through. “Setup cancelled or expired” reports retired setups. Context reversal and zone-change conditions are also available.

The script’s detailed alert stream includes feed, timeframe pair, event, reference and settings. “Detailed alerts” switches control that stream; they do not disable the individual named alert conditions. Setup and price-trigger events are enabled in the stream by default. No orders are sent.

How does this fit with Time Map V1.2?

Time Map: when to watch for a turn. Momentum: whether its context, pullback, reversal and follow-through rules align. On Daily, the momentum context is Weekly.

There is no automatic connection between the scripts. For a combined test, capture every momentum setup and whether it falls inside an already-issued Time Map window. Keep the outside-window cases for comparison; do not assume the combination improves results.

What does Show confirmed context lines add?

It displays the confirmed higher-timeframe fast and slow lines as steps. The ribbon and table already summarize that context. Turning this display on does not change setup calculations.

Keep the distinction.
Permission ≠ setup. Setup ≠ trigger. Trigger ≠ profitable trade.
