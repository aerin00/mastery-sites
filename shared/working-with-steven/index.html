Working with me · Steven Nguyen

What to expect, and what I need from you

Two assessments, taken six years apart, agree on one thing about how I work. This is that finding and what to do with it. Three minutes.

The finding

I open questions naturally. I close them by effort.

My strongest habits expand a problem: asking, testing assumptions, weighing other views. The habits that close a problem down, committing to a date and sequencing the steps, rank last of seven. Expect strong framing and diagnosis early. Expect me to need a forcing function to land it.

Why I lean on checklists and templates. I hold high standards for correctness but step-sequencing is not a natural habit for me, so I build the structure deliberately. It is scaffolding, not bureaucracy.

Point me at

  • Problems that are not fully defined yet
  • Root cause work and assumption testing
  • Checking whether a plan holds up before we commit
  • Technical research and evaluating options
  • Work that has to be right the first time

Expect friction on

  • Committing to a date before I feel ready
  • Producing a rough first pass
  • Step-by-step delivery plans
  • Arguing my case in a fast-moving meeting
  • Telling you my own work is going well

Six things that make me easier to manage

Give me the decision date, not just the delivery date.
"We decide Thursday" moves me. An open deadline does not.
Ask for my recommendation, not my thoughts.
"What do you think" gets more exploration. "What do you recommend, and why" gets a decision.
Send it in writing first.
Agenda or materials ahead of the meeting gets you a considered answer instead of an instant one.
If I go quiet, I disagree.
I will not push back hard in the room. Ask me again in writing and you will get the real answer.
Bring me problems early.
Before they are fully defined is where I add the most.
Ask for the rough version, and mean it.
My failure mode is refining past the point of value. Interrupting that is a favour, not a slight.

Four things you will probably read wrong at first

When I go quiet

It is not agreement. It is unspoken disagreement. Ask again, ideally in writing.

When I build a checklist or template

It is not process for its own sake. It is scaffolding for my weakest habit.

When I do not promote my own work

It is not a lack of ownership. I understate by default. Ask me directly what I own.

When I slow down on a decision

It is not risk aversion. I am testing whether the assumptions hold. Give me a date and I will land it.
