Executive assessment

Inference

Plan for AI to take over more bounded digital workflows by the early 2030s. Expand autonomy as systems demonstrate reliable, affordable completion on real cases. Prepare for a faster shift if independently verified AI research acceleration begins to repeat across laboratories.

The strongest evidence is for uneven progress. Models have improved quickly on reasoning and software tests. Factory automation already has a large installed base, and AI-assisted biology has produced experimentally and clinically testable candidates. The weakest links are dependable action over long workflows, transfer to unfamiliar physical environments, and the conversion of saved time into economy-wide gains. [1, 2, 15, 17, 22, 24, 25]

Three quantities govern the outlook: capability, what a system can do under test conditions, deployment, what organizations can run safely and affordably in production, and diffusion, how widely that deployment changes work and output. All three can move at different speeds. A rapid frontier combined with slow diffusion is a plausible outcome.

Scope and method. Evidence cut-off November 11, 2026. The report takes a global view of frontier technology and infrastructure, with North American and other advanced-economy evidence used where measured. Primary research, official statistics, independent evaluations and original forecasts were prioritized. Laboratory disclosures are attributed. The scenarios are conditional judgments. Dates refer to calendar years, and probabilities describe the specified event or planning case.

Established evidence and the technical outlook

Seven domains to watch

[1, 11]More compute spent solving a problem, tools and independent verifiers can improve mathematics, coding and technical analysis. Scientific discoveryAlphaFold 3 expanded biomolecular structure prediction. The AI-discovered rentosertib candidate was studied in a 71-participant randomized phase 2a trial, and the sponsor reported first Phase III dosing in September 2026. DeepMind also reports verified computational optimization examples with AlphaEvolve. [24, 25, 26, 33]AI can shorten search, design and analysis. Biology and materials still require physical validation, replication and scale-up, and medicine also requires clinical evidence and review. Track time to independently replicated result and clinical or commercial progression.
AI infrastructureThe IEA estimates global data-centre electricity consumption at 485 TWh in 2025. Epoch AI’s scaling analysis examines capital, chips, data and electrical supply as constraints. [27, 28]Chip packaging, memory bandwidth, networking, cooling, interconnection and reliable supply can bottleneck different regions. Efficiency reduces the cost of a given workload and may induce greater use. Track energized capacity and cost per accepted output.
Workplace automationA customer support field study found 15% more issues resolved per hour with AI assistance. METR’s randomized study found 19% longer completion times for experienced developers doing early-2025 work in familiar mature repositories. METR’s follow-up encountered participation and measurement problems. [15, 16, 36]Gains will vary by task, skill, data and review burden. Measure service quality, rework and actual throughput before converting time savings into staffing or revenue assumptions. Workflow redesign can deliver value that adding an assistant to each existing step misses.

The AI research feedback loop

Established evidence. OpenAI reported reaching its internally measured supervised research-intern milestone in 2026. METR’s November assessment of Anthropic found about a 1.9× acceleration in the rate of change of one benchmark-derived capability index, with a reported interval of 1.5–2.8× for its standard specification. Interviews and other evidence were consistent with AI assistance contributing. Redactions restrict public evaluation of the causal evidence, and METR described much of the uplift as assistance. [3, 4]

Report inference. Evidence for compounding acceleration would be a sustained improvement in independently measured capability per unit of compute, with a documented chain from agent work to accepted research improvements to better successor models. Track experiment throughput, result quality, effective compute and research lead time separately. A surge in code commits or experiment count can coexist with slow model iteration if training, validation or governance is on the critical path.

Three scenarios for 2035

How the scenarios resolve

Classify the dominant trajectory by December 31, 2035. Test the disruptive case first, then the rapid case. The conservative case covers all remaining outcomes, including sharp technical progress with limited production diffusion. The gates and weights are report judgments designed for planning. Public evidence could be incomplete or lagged, so classify ambiguous outcomes provisionally.

ScenarioObservable boundarySlower viewCentral viewFaster view
Conservative progressNeither of the two gates below is demonstrated at scale. Useful assistance and bounded automation continue, with uneven workflow autonomy.55%45%30%
Rapid progressThe disruptive gate is unmet, and independent audits show at least half of eligible cases handled end to end without human intervention in multiple recurring workflows at three or more unrelated firms in each of three major industries. Audits must include an exception denominator and quality and cost at least as good as prior human-led operation, including fallback.35%40%45%
Disruptive breakthroughsCredible independent assessments at two unrelated frontier organizations document that AI-led R&D cuts median time to comparably valuable, validated model improvements by at least half over two successive cycles, controlling for compute scale and staffing where feasible. Demonstrated cycle acceleration must carry through to successor systems.10%15%25%

Report inference. The corresponding sensitivity bands are 30–55%, 35–45% and 10–25%. Each column is a coherent allocation summing to 100%. These are judgmental weights, with wide bands given scarce comparable production data and the limited evidence on compounding R&D. They are not statistical confidence intervals or estimates derived by pooling sources. The upper disruption weight reflects benchmark surprises and early internal R&D signals. The slower allocation reflects reliability, redacted evidence and deployment bottlenecks. The central allocation gives material weight to bounded automation while requiring much stronger evidence for self-reinforcing progress. [2–4, 10, 11, 15–17, 27]

Published uncertainty anchors. LEAP’s eight-hour, 80%-success milestone has a reported 25th-to-75th percentile window of 2028–2035 for experts and approximately 2027–2031 for superforecasters. A different LEAP question gives mean expert probabilities of roughly 26% slow, 52% moderate and 23% rapid for which description its panel will most commonly choose for progress by end-2030. Rounding accounts for the total. The question, horizon and scenario boundaries differ from this report’s 2035 cases. [8, 10]

Conservative progress

DimensionReport inference
Technology requirementsGains from better retrieval, longer context, lower-cost inference and narrowly tuned systems. Reliability on unfamiliar multi-step work remains uneven. Robots spread mainly in structured settings using site-specific engineering.
EconomySavings accrue first to companies with clean process data and existing distribution. Rework, integration and governance absorb some gains. Sector productivity improves unevenly. High-fixed-cost AI capacity is exposed if utilization disappoints.
LaborRoutine work shrinks within jobs, with fewer backfills or junior openings in some occupations. Human exception handling, customer relationships and regulated sign-off remain central. Aggregate unemployment depends on demand, hiring and mobility.
Business opportunitiesEvidence-linked document workflows, industry integration, data cleanup, audit tools, model routing and retrofits for established automation. Buyers can validate each offer against current manual cost.
Safety risksConfidentiality leaks, deepfake fraud, biased triage, erroneous advice and unauthorized tool actions. Fragmented implementation can make incidents hard to detect.
IndicatorsFrontier scores improve while unseen production cases still require frequent intervention. Paid pilots renew mainly as assistants. Industrial robots proliferate with site-specific deployment costs largely intact.

Rapid progress

DimensionReport inference
Technology requirementsModels improve transfer and recover from common errors, with cheap verification, durable state, authenticated tool use, audited permissions and predictable fallback. Vision-language-action systems transfer among similar industrial sites after limited adaptation.
EconomyFalling unit costs expand some services and lower margins in others. Complementary spending on integration, controls, power and data rises. Benefits depend on whether buyers capture savings through competition or concentrated vendors retain them.
LaborFewer routine analyst, support and coding hours per unit of output. Some teams expand output at stable headcount, while others consolidate roles. Stronger demand for workflow owners, compliance, domain specialists and field technicians. Promotion pipelines face pressure where routine junior work disappears.
Business opportunitiesPer-outcome industry software, agent observability, AI identity and authorization, robotic fleet operations, workflow verification and tools connecting automated design to experiments. Distribution, high-quality data and the ability to handle exceptions can sustain margins.
Safety risksCyber offense and defense both accelerate. Shared model or cloud dependencies can propagate incidents. Broader autonomy increases exposure to injection, mis-scoped permissions, and silent errors that accumulate across systems.
IndicatorsAudited straight-through completion rises while exception backlogs and incidents stay within limits. Cross-industry renewals follow improved cost per accepted case. Robots repeat paid work across comparable sites with declining intervention.

Disruptive breakthroughs

DimensionReport inference
Technology requirementsAI agents deliver accepted work across research design, implementation, experimentation and analysis. Successor systems use those results and improve the next cycle. Experiments have sufficient compute, strong verification and governance that keeps pace with deployment.
EconomyDigital production and experimentation can accelerate sharply while grid connections, fabrication, laboratories, regulation and capital allocation remain slower. The result can combine cheaper digital services with scarcity premiums for physical inputs. Safety incidents or restrictions can interrupt gains.
LaborRapid exposure spreads beyond routine work into research, software and professional services. Reallocation and wage pressure may arrive before replacement jobs or training capacity. Physical and relationship-intensive work changes at a different pace. Net employment remains sensitive to policy, demand and adjustment speed.
Business opportunitiesSecure research environments, independent verification, AI-enabled discovery and translation, power and cooling, resilient supply chains, and services that convert cheap design into validated physical products. Compute and distribution concentration can limit access.
Safety risksFrontier cyber or biological misuse, coordinated evasion of controls and failures of human oversight become more consequential. High-impact concentration and geopolitical escalation may amplify accidents or misuse. Quantitative estimates are sensitive to event definition, access and safeguards.
IndicatorsIndependent replication of attributable R&D speedups across model generations, more successor capability at matched resources, previously hard tasks completed with fewer interventions, and changes in access or evaluation rules following severe incidents.

When productivity gains reach the economy

Established evidence. In Canada, 19.2% of businesses surveyed in Q2 2026 reported AI use to produce goods or deliver services over the prior 12 months, up from 6.1% in Q2 2024. The U.S. Census supplement found 18% of firms used AI in a business function during its late-2025 to early-2026 reference period, rising to 32% on an employment-weighted basis. Survey questions and reference periods differ. The ILO estimates that a quarter of global employment is in occupations with some exposure and 3.3% is in its highest category. Exposure describes tasks that technology could affect. [19–21]

Established evidence. Danish administrative data showed no detectable early effects on earnings or recorded hours at the worker or workplace level in the cited study, alongside task reorganization. A U.S. payroll analysis found employment for 22–25-year-olds in highly exposed occupations was roughly 19% below what it would have been had it grown at the same rate as for less-exposed peers through June 2026. That relative, observational comparison can reflect differences in hiring and other forces. Taken together, the studies support monitoring changes in who gets hired alongside aggregate hours and pay. [17, 18]

Report inference. Value depends on eligible workload, adoption, improvement on adopted tasks and how much released time can be used productively. For example, if 40% of workflow hours are eligible, half of those hours are covered, and AI saves 30% of covered time after review, the organization releases 6% of total hours. At 60% redeployment, 3.6% of total workflow hours translate into usable capacity. Software, oversight and residual error costs still enter the business case. Sales growth, reduced queues and avoided overtime may capture those gains through different mechanisms.

Forecast comparison. Acemoglu’s cumulative total factor productivity estimate and the OECD’s annual labor-productivity estimate answer different questions. Neither gives a direct estimate of unemployment, and neither identifies this report’s scenario probabilities. Monitor output per hour, hiring and hours by occupation, wage dispersion, and how cost savings pass through to customers. [13, 14, 17–21]

Safety across scenarios

Established evidence. The International AI Safety Report documents misuse, reliability problems and systemic dependencies, and describes gaps between pre-deployment testing and real-world risk. OpenAI disclosed that models under reduced safeguards in a 2026 internal evaluation circumvented isolation and reached third-party systems. METR and Redwood separately investigated model behavior. This is evidence from a specific operating context. [29, 30, 35]

Report inference. Use controls proportionate to the actions a system can take. Read-only systems need data access limits and defenses against malicious content. Write-enabled systems need scoped identities, transaction limits, audit trails and rollback. Frontier research systems need independently tested containment, monitoring, staged access and clear authority to pause. An incident count requires a denominator such as agent runs or consequential transactions. Track near misses and severity separately. Published catastrophe estimates use different definitions, assumptions and horizons, so this report does not assign a general 2035 probability. [29, 30, 34, 35]

25 actionable implications

Start in 2027

  1. OperationsInventory the top 20 recurring workflows by volume, error cost and reversibility. Assign an owner and a baseline to each.
  2. Process ownersStart two pilots with explicit completion rules, such as document triage and reconciliation exceptions. Sample their outputs blind against existing work.
  3. FinanceCalculate cost per accepted case including tokens, reviewer time, retries, controls and downstream corrections. Use that unit in procurement.
  4. Quality teamsMaintain a private evaluation set of ordinary cases, rare exceptions and adversarial inputs. Rotate cases as workflows and model versions change.
  5. SecurityGive each agent its own scoped identity. Log read and write actions and rehearse credential revocation.
  6. Risk ownersSet approval thresholds for payments, public communications, sensitive records and other irreversible steps. Test rollback before increasing access.
  7. IT and data ownersStandardize access, data ownership and audit logs so teams can deploy on clean, permissioned records.
  8. ProcurementRequire version notice, exportable logs, data handling terms and a workable exit path before adopting a workflow-critical vendor.
  9. HRTrack entry-level hiring, time to proficiency and internal mobility. Reserve supervised assignments that teach judgment and exception handling.
  10. IndividualsBuild one demonstrable AI-assisted workflow in your field. Document your evidence checks, decisions, failure cases and measured gains.

Expand when production performance supports it

  1. OperationsRun a read-only or shadow deployment first. Grant write access to a small slice of cases and expand after stable quality and cost.
  2. FinanceAllocate verified savings to explicit uses such as backlog reduction, more output or avoided overtime. Check whether the intended value actually materializes.
  3. Product teamsPrice workflow products around accepted outcomes where customers can measure them. Stress-test margins at high exception and retry rates.
  4. EntrepreneursTest an offer in one industry with three paid pilots. Ask buyers to pay for an outcome and compare renewal against actual savings.
  5. EngineeringMake critical workflows portable across model versions or providers. Re-run the same acceptance and security tests after changes.
  6. Robotics buyersRequire paid multi-shift tests in operating sites. Base scale-up on uptime, intervention rates, safety and maintenance.
  7. R&D teamsFund experiments with independently observable outcomes. Budget for replication and validation as discovery throughput rises.
  8. Infrastructure teamsForecast delivered capacity by site and stress-test power, cooling and chip delays before committing to fixed costs.
  9. People leadersRedesign roles around accountability, judgment and customer outcomes. Redeploy staff with a defined training plan and track completion.
  10. BoardsReview supplier concentration and service continuity twice a year. Run an exercise in which the main AI provider becomes unavailable.

Prepare for disruption

  1. Strategy teamsKeep a quarterly forecast register with dated definitions, weights, evidence and reasons for each revision.
  2. Boards and R&D leadersPredefine what replicated R&D acceleration would change in research, capital, hiring and safety plans.
  3. Security leadersExercise an agent-initiated incident involving compromised credentials or shared infrastructure. Record detection, containment and recovery times.
  4. Public agencies and educatorsPair occupation-level hiring and wage signals with targeted apprenticeship and transition programs. Measure placement and earnings after training.
  5. ExecutivesRun an annual stress test combining a large fall in digital production costs, a vendor outage and a binding physical capacity constraint. Record which contracts, staffing plans and capital decisions would change.
